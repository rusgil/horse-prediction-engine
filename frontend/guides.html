Understand Australian racing, one page at a time

A race card can look like a wall of numbers and abbreviations. These plain-English guides break down what every figure means, why barriers and track conditions change a race, and how we turn all of it into a probability. No jargon left unexplained.

Australian thoroughbred racing runs to its own conventions — a 1–10 track rating, weight-carrying handicaps, barrier draws, gear changes and a shorthand form line that packs a horse's last ten runs into a single string of digits. Once you can read those signals, a race card stops being noise and starts telling a story about who is well-placed to win and who is quietly up against it.

Start with whichever guide matches what's in front of you. If you're staring at a field for the first time, begin with the race-card walkthrough. If a horse's price surprised you, the barrier and track-condition guides usually explain why.
